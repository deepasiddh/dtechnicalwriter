A Weekend at Isha Yoga Center, Coimbatore

📅 Traveled in: Month, Year

We decided to spend our parent’s 40th Wedding Anniversary at a place of my mom’s preference. She chose Isha Yoga Center near Coimbutore. So, we booked a three-day stay at the ISHA Yoga Center and travelled to Coimbutore by train. From Coimbutore, we took a cab to Isha Yoga center.

Little did we know that a peaceful and spiritual stay was waiting for us!

Conveniently located at the foothills of the Velliangiri Mountains, the center spans across 150 acres land. The center is part of the Nilgiri Biosphere, a reserve forest with abundant wildlife. The rooms we booked were simple, clean, and elegant. Wherever you turn, you see mountains, trees and lush greenery. There is no TV, of course! No soaps No movies :) Only BSNL phones showed signal range; Mobiles using other operators did not show any range. Lesser radiation ;)

The focal point of the center is the Dhyanalinga Yogic Temple. I was surprised at the way the ISHA volunteers and staff manage a huge crowd without raising their voice but depicting silence through their actions. The visitors quietly wait for their turn to enter into the temple. Amazing! The huge statue of Nandhi is built near the entrance of the temple. It is a metal structure but has the finish of a stone statue. Royal indeed! Two important places you must not miss during your visit to the center are the Theerthakund and Linga Bhairavi temple:

Theerthakund is a sacred water body designed for the use of the public. Resembling a rectangular water tank (Suryakund for men and Chandrakund for women) framed by gigantic granite stones, the Theerthakund is embedded at a depth of 30 feet. Visitors generally take a dip before entering temples in the past generations. Such act was considered to cleanse and freshen the visitors however, it was stopped over time due to various reasons. As per Sadguru, this act activates and prepares the visitors to fully receive the energies and the grace. Visitors are encouraged to use sadhvik dresses and climb down stone steps to the water body. As you enter the theerthakund, dip in the vibrant water, and touch the sacred stone, you are sure to experience an unforgettable spiritual feeling. Do not miss the spectacular, hand painted murals on the curved ceiling above! It seems the water is energized by a submerged Rasalinga (Badarasa means Mercury) that increases our spiritual receptivity and rejuvenates us. Quite True!

Lingabhairavi temple houses the idol of holy goddess Lingabhairavi. It resembles any local temple excluding the fact that we have priestesses and donations are not accepted. However, you can buy few items such as flowers and oil candles from the stall outside the temple and offer them if interested.

We also visited a pond near Suryakund full of lotuses. We saw a bird walking on lotus leaves to catch fish. Eyecatchy :) FYI, there were restrictions on taking photos within the center.

During our whole stay, both adults and children in our group felt the peace and harmony and it was a completely different experience for us. My parents were very happy to spend their valuable time with their daughters and son and their families in the way they wanted… Life at Peace! Rushing back to the city for our train to Chennai was another story, not as interesting though!
